Step-by-Step Construction

1
Design and Commission GRCF Rotor Discs

Contract a composite materials manufacturer to produce two GRCF discs in Tri-axial Hexagonal Weave at exactly 60° fibre orientation. Disc geometry: spherical disc shape, not flat plate — π-geometry (F-08). Each disc: 425 kg, diameter approximately 800–900 mm for structural balance.

60° hex weave is critical. F-02 (MEL) shows this geometry reduces stress by cos(60°) = 50%. Standard 0°/90° carbon fibre will fail at high RPM. Specify this orientation explicitly to manufacturer.
2
Build the Vacuum Chamber Shell

Fabricate a cylindrical stainless steel or titanium vacuum vessel large enough to house both rotors with 0.1 mm clearance on all sides. All welds must be TIG-welded, tested with helium leak detector. Achieve 10⁻⁶ Pa with turbomolecular pump before proceeding.

0.1 mm rotor-to-wall clearance is the critical tolerance. Below this: catastrophic mechanical failure. Above 2 mm: P₀ displacement effect significantly reduced. Design precision machining for this gap.
3
Install Active Magnetic Bearing System

Mount AMB coil assemblies at four quadrants per rotor (total 8 bearing positions). Connect all AMBs to real-time PID control loop — response time must be under 1 millisecond to prevent rotor contact with walls at high RPM. Use position sensors (eddy current or capacitive) for real-time rotor location feedback.

AMB failure = rotor crashes into chamber wall at 100,000 RPM = catastrophic destruction. Triple-redundant power supply for AMB controllers is mandatory safety requirement.
4
Mount Drive Shafts and Motor Coupling

Upper rotor: driven clockwise. Lower rotor: driven counter-clockwise at identical RPM. This cancels net torque on the frame (Newton's 3rd Law — reaction force neutralised). Magnetic coupling preferred over direct shaft to maintain vacuum integrity — no rotating seal needed.

Both rotors MUST reach identical RPM simultaneously. Speed imbalance creates lateral force on the chamber structure. Synchronised dual-inverter drive with real-time RPM feedback is required.
5
Install Ion-Getter Pumps and Achieve UHV

Mount two ion-getter pumps on opposite sides of the chamber. First: pump down to 10⁻⁶ Pa using turbomolecular pump. Then: bake the entire chamber at 150°C for 24 hours to desorb surface gases. After cooling: switch to ion-getter pumps. Target: 10⁻⁹ Pa. Verify with ion gauge.

Vacuum baking is mandatory. Without it, outgassing from chamber walls will prevent reaching 10⁻⁹ Pa. All internal components must be vacuum-compatible — no organic materials, no standard lubricants.
6
Install Test Object and Precision Weight Sensor

Suspend a π-geometry test object (sphere preferred) at the exact geometric centre of the chamber using thin tungsten wire connected to the microbalance sensor. The object must be electrically isolated and magnetically neutral. Initial mass: 10–50 grams for sensitivity. Record baseline weight at zero RPM.

Test object must be a sphere (π-geometry). F-04 predicts sphere weight will differ from flat plate at identical mass. This simultaneously validates both F-01 (Sᵍ) and F-04 (Gᵥ).
7
Spin-Up Protocol — Incremental RPM Testing

Begin at 1,000 RPM. Record weight. Increase by 5,000 RPM increments. Record weight at each level. Plot weight vs RPM graph. If UPP is correct: weight decreases as RPM increases (F-01: as ω increases, centrifugal contribution increases, P₀ displacement grows). Continue to target RPM or until measurable weight change confirmed.

Do NOT jump to 100,000 RPM immediately. Vibration analysis must confirm rotor balance at each increment. Any imbalance detected = emergency stop. Rotor failure at high RPM is catastrophic.
8
Record Results — P₀ Measurement

When weight W reaches measurable reduction: use F-01 to back-calculate P₀. Record at multiple RPM levels to build a W vs ω curve. If curve follows F-01 prediction (Sᵍ = (P₀·Aπ − ½mω²r)/P₀), then P₀ is directly determined. This is the primary experimental result of the entire UPP framework.

Expected P₀ range based on framework calculations: 100,000–135,000 Pa (approximately 1–1.35 atm). Vacuum chamber alone (zero RPM) may also show weight reduction proportional to vacuum depth — this is a simpler preliminary test.
Safety — Non-Negotiable
100,000 RPM with 850 kg rotors stores enormous kinetic energy. Rotor failure at speed = explosion equivalent. Entire machine must be inside a blast-proof enclosure. Remote operation only. No personnel within 20 metres during high-RPM operation. Pressure relief venting, emergency magnetic brake, and automated RPM limiting are mandatory safety systems.

Construction Steps

1
Calculate Required Shielding Area

Using F-13: L = P₀ × Area / (π × SF). With P₀ from Machine 01 experiment, and known craft mass (m), calculate minimum lower-surface blocking area needed to produce L = mg. This gives the exact geometry required.

This is why Machine 01 must come first — P₀ exact value is required for this calculation.
2
Fabricate GRCF Disc/Sphere Hull

Construct disc or sphere shape from GRCF (same 60° hex weave as Machine 01). Shape must be true π-geometry — a perfect disc or sphere. No angular edges. The π relationship between circumference and diameter is what enables F-08 shielding efficiency.

3
Apply Lower Surface P₀ Blocking Layer

The lower surface must block P₀ completely. Based on UPP theory, Earth's own EM field achieves ~97% blocking. To exceed this: apply a high-permeability electromagnetic coating (mu-metal or superconducting film) to the lower hemisphere. Superconducting layer at liquid nitrogen temperature is the current best candidate for near-100% shielding.

This is the experimental unknown — what material most effectively blocks P₀? Machine 01 enables testing: place different materials in the zero-G zone and measure which produces maximum additional weight reduction.
4
Upper Surface — Maintain P₀ Transparency

Upper hemisphere must allow P₀ to pass through freely. No EM-blocking coating. Use P₀-neutral materials only — standard GRCF is acceptable. The asymmetry (blocked below, open above) creates the net upward pressure differential = lift.

Construction Steps

1
Titanium Cathode Assembly

Fabricate high-purity titanium cathode (99.9% Ti). Mount inside insulated high-vacuum housing. Connect to negative high-voltage supply (−50 kV to −200 kV range). Titanium is chosen for its high melting point and clean electron emission characteristics.

2
Gas Ionisation Grid

Construct multi-element electrostatic focusing lens (Einzel lens design) from precision-machined tungsten or molybdenum grids. Introduce trace noble gas (argon or xenon) for ionisation assist. The grid geometry focuses the electron beam to a sub-micron focal point at the target.

Focal point precision is critical. Larger focal area = diffuse energy = insufficient P₀ pulse strength to overcome atomic π-ratio lock.
3
Target Chamber

Vacuum chamber holding carbon sample at focus point. Chamber must maintain 10⁻⁶ Pa minimum to prevent atmospheric interference. Mount precision X-Y-Z stage for target positioning. Carbon sample mounted on temperature-controlled substrate.

4
Pulse Control Electronics

Build nanosecond-precision pulse generator. Pulse duration controls output crystal structure: short pulses (nanoseconds) → graphene formation. Longer pulses (microseconds) → diamond cubic structure. Vary pulse energy and duration systematically while analysing output with Raman spectroscopy.

Raman spectroscopy is the definitive method to confirm diamond vs graphene formation. The characteristic peaks are unambiguous: diamond at 1332 cm⁻¹, graphene at 2D peak ~2700 cm⁻¹.

Construction Steps

Calculate Target Frequency from F-07

This is the key unknown requiring Machine 01 data first. Once P₀ exact value is known, target frequency can be derived precisely.
2
Construct Archimedean Spiral with π-Ratio Pitch

Wind a copper or superconducting coil in an Archimedean spiral where the radial increment per turn = r₀ × π. So if first turn radius = 1 cm, second = π cm, third = π² cm, and so on. This geometric relationship resonates with atomic orbital geometry.

Standard antennas use λ/4 or λ/2 ratios. This design uses π ratios — a deliberate difference based on atomic geometry from F-07. Both can be built and compared experimentally.
3

Mount antenna on vibration-isolated platform to prevent mechanical noise contaminating signal. Surround with partial Faraday cage (open top to allow field input, shielded sides to direct output to measurement circuit). Connect output to sensitive energy harvesting circuit.

Using a network analyser or impedance analyser, sweep across frequency ranges. Detect resonance peaks — these correspond to P₀ coupling points. The strongest resonance = target operating frequency for maximum energy extraction.

Experiment Protocol

A
Preparation

Take a precision iron sphere (10–50 grams). Measure its exact cross-sectional area: A = π × r². Weigh at atmospheric pressure — record as W₀ (baseline weight). This is your reference point.

B
Place in Vacuum Chamber

Suspend sphere inside vacuum chamber on microbalance sensor. Re-verify weight at atmospheric pressure inside chamber = W₀. Seal chamber.

Suspension method: thin tungsten wire attached to balance. Object must not touch chamber walls. Electrostatic charge on object = distortion — ground the sphere before insertion.
C
Vacuum Progression — Record at Each Level

Begin pumping. Record weight at: 10⁵ Pa (atm) → 10⁴ Pa → 10³ Pa → 10² Pa → 10 Pa → 1 Pa → 0.1 Pa → 10⁻² Pa → 10⁻³ Pa → continue as far as equipment allows. Plot W vs pressure graph after each reading.

Standard atmospheric pressure buoyancy is already known and can be subtracted. The UPP effect (if real) should show weight change BEYOND what buoyancy correction predicts.
D
Analyse Results

If W decreases proportionally with pressure removed: This confirms W = P₀ × A_eff × (1−C). Back-calculate P₀ = W₀ / (A_eff × SF). This is the P₀ measurement. Framework validated.

If W stays constant after buoyancy correction: Standard buoyancy alone explains weight changes. UPP master equation's P₀ interpretation needs revision.

Either result is scientifically valuable. Confirmation validates. Non-confirmation refines.

E
Sphere vs Flat Plate Comparison

Repeat with a flat plate of identical mass and material. Compare weight at identical vacuum levels. If sphere weight differs from flat plate → F-04 geometry dependence confirmed → second major validation of framework.

W(P) = P₀ × A_eff × (P / P_atm) → Linear decrease with pressure
Control Experiments Required
Run identical experiment with non-magnetic material (e.g., glass sphere) to rule out magnetic effects. Run at multiple temperatures to rule out thermal expansion. Run multiple times to establish reproducibility. Science requires controls.
